Online personalization under action-dependent feedback.

We evaluate how a small language model can adapt online when each request permits one action and reveals only the outcome produced by that action.

Online-SDFT Demo 12 min read LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M

Summary

We compare six ways to adapt a real 230M-parameter Liquid language model during a drifting stream of notifications. Unlike a batch dataset, every decision changes what can be observed next. Online-SDFT lets the student act without privileged information, then distills a post-decision teacher’s distribution in tiny LoRA updates. Across three paired streams, it reaches 64.72% online accuracy and 36.24 cumulative regret—measured while learning, not after.

01 · Action-dependent feedback

Notification routing produces partially observed outcomes.

Consider a social notification that arrives during an on-call shift. If the router archives it, no notification is displayed. A later organic app open is observable, but the response to an immediate push or delayed digest is not.

A supervised dataset may associate each context with a preferred route, implicitly treating all alternatives as observable. In deployment, the policy selects one route, the environment executes it, and outcomes under the unselected routes remain counterfactual.

Why it matters Training on a click associated with an unexecuted route would introduce counterfactual leakage into the learner.

The learner must not receive outcomes associated with actions it did not execute.

Causal information constraint

The environment therefore simulates sequential interaction rather than performing a labeled-row lookup. It reveals the context, accepts one action, samples only that action’s outcome, and advances the stream. Evaluation uses the decisions served by the policy before each update.

03 · The formal problem

Contextual-bandit formulation.

At round t, the router observes a context xt, chooses one action at, and receives only the outcome caused by that action. Then the next notification arrives.

xtobserve→ at ∼ π(·|xt)commit→ ot(at), rtone outcome→ πt+1update

The evaluator may simulate utilities for all three actions to compute regret, but those values stay behind a strict information boundary. They never enter the student, its prompt, its memory, or the teacher.

Online accuracy

Was the route chosen before learning on this round the evaluator’s best route?

Cumulative regret

How much simulated utility was lost versus the best route, summed over the stream?

Prequential evaluation Prequential evaluation measures service quality during adaptation: each action is scored before its feedback or update. The protocol has no held-out test set and does not replay requests.

04 · Online-SDFT

Student action precedes teacher feedback.

Standard distillation may begin with a privileged demonstration and ask a student to imitate it. That ordering would violate this setting’s serving-time information boundary. Online-SDFT instead requires the unprivileged student to generate its route first; the teacher receives permitted post-decision evidence second; and a small LoRA adapter updates third.

for x_t in stream:
    a_t = student.rollout(x_t)              # no privileged z
    score_pre_update(a_t)                   # evaluator only; never leaked
    feedback_t = execute_only(a_t)          # one factual outcome
    q_t = teacher(x_t, a_t, z_t, feedback_t)
    student.update_small_batch(q_t)         # soft target, online

The teacher distribution qt contains more information than a scalar reward but does not expose the evaluator’s oracle. It may assign most probability to later, some to archive, and little to interrupt, enabling an update even when there is no explicit user reward.

Why does reward-only REINFORCE underperform?

REINFORCE receives one scalar from one rollout. In this simulator, archival silence often produces zero reward, while an intrusive push can produce negative reward. The policy increasingly selects archive, a low-variance action that reduces effective exploration and accumulates evaluator regret. Online-SDFT can update from the teacher distribution even when there is no explicit user response.

07 · Interpretation

Scope and limitations.

Supported

On these three paired simulator streams, Online-SDFT improves the quality of the decisions made during adaptation.

Supported

The implementation preserves the causal order: student rollout, one factual outcome, teacher, then online update.

Not yet established

Three seeds are not enough for a broad claim about all users, devices, or drifting environments.

Not yet established

The simulator’s utility weights are a declared product model, not a universal measure of notification quality.

Further validation requires more paired online streams, ablations of the teacher and reward design, and a carefully instrumented real interaction study.
