An interactive research note · August 2026

The notification you didn’t send has no click.

How can a small language model personalize itself online when it gets one action, one outcome, and no second chance? We built a causal notification-routing stream to find out.

Online-SDFT Demo 12 min read LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M

In one paragraph

We compare six ways to adapt a real 230M-parameter Liquid language model during a drifting stream of notifications. Unlike a batch dataset, every decision changes what can be observed next. Online-SDFT lets the student act without privileged information, then distills a post-decision teacher’s distribution in tiny LoRA updates. Across three paired streams, it reaches 64.72% online accuracy and 36.24 cumulative regret—measured while learning, not after.

01 · The missing outcome

Silence is an action, not a label.

Suppose your phone receives a social notification during an on-call shift. The router chooses archive, so nothing appears. Ten minutes later, the user opens the app. Was the silent choice good? Perhaps. But we cannot also know whether an immediate alert would have been clicked, dismissed, or resented.

A normal supervised dataset often stores a row and a “correct” route. That makes every route look observable. Production is harsher: the model chooses one route, reality executes it, and the alternatives become counterfactuals.

Why it matters Training on the click attached to an unsent notification would leak an impossible alternate outcome into the learner.

There is no click for the notification you did not send.

The causal constraint behind the benchmark

Our environment therefore simulates an interaction, not a table lookup. It reveals the context, accepts exactly one action, samples only that action’s outcome, and then moves on. The student is judged on the decisions it actually served.

03 · The formal problem

A contextual bandit in one line.

At round t, the router observes a context xt, chooses one action at, and receives only the outcome caused by that action. Then the next notification arrives.

xtobserve→ at ∼ π(·|xt)commit→ ot(at), rtone outcome→ πt+1update

The evaluator may simulate utilities for all three actions to compute regret, but those values stay behind a strict information boundary. They never enter the student, its prompt, its memory, or the teacher.

Online accuracy

Was the route chosen before learning on this round the evaluator’s best route?

Cumulative regret

How much simulated utility was lost versus the best route, summed over the stream?

Prequential evaluation “Predict, then learn” measures service quality during adaptation. There is no held-out test set and no replay of the same request.

04 · Online-SDFT

Let the student act. Let the teacher explain afterward.

Typical distillation often begins with a privileged demonstration and asks a student to imitate it. That would cheat here. Online-SDFT reverses the order: the unprivileged student rolls out its own route first; a teacher sees legal post-decision evidence second; a tiny LoRA adapter updates third.

for x_t in stream:
    a_t = student.rollout(x_t)              # no privileged z
    score_pre_update(a_t)                   # evaluator only; never leaked
    feedback_t = execute_only(a_t)          # one factual outcome
    q_t = teacher(x_t, a_t, z_t, feedback_t)
    student.update_small_batch(q_t)         # soft target, online

The teacher distribution qt is richer than a scalar reward but weaker than an oracle. It can say, for example, “later is probably right, archive is plausible, interrupt is unlikely,” allowing learning even when a user gives no explicit reward.

Why does reward-only REINFORCE collapse?

REINFORCE receives one scalar from one rollout. In this simulator, archival silence is often a harmless zero, while an intrusive push can be negative. The policy increasingly chooses archive: locally safe-looking behavior that stalls exploration and accumulates evaluator regret. Online-SDFT can still learn from descriptive teacher feedback when the user does nothing.

07 · Interpretation

What this experiment does—and does not—show.

Supported

On these three paired simulator streams, Online-SDFT improves the quality of the decisions made during adaptation.

Supported

The implementation preserves the causal order: student rollout, one factual outcome, teacher, then online update.

Not yet established

Three seeds are not enough for a broad claim about all users, devices, or drifting environments.

Not yet established

The simulator’s utility weights are a declared product model, not a universal measure of notification quality.

The right next step is not a larger held-out test set. It is a larger collection of paired online streams, ablations of the teacher and reward design, and eventually a carefully instrumented real interaction study.
